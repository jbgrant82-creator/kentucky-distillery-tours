How the 0–100 composite score is built

Two components: what visitors say (70%) and what critics, judges and the press say (30%). Every input is public and listed on the Sources tab.

Step 1 — Bayesian shrinkage

Each platform rating is pulled toward that platform’s own volume-weighted mean, so a 5.0 from nine reviews cannot outrank a 4.8 from three thousand.

adjusted = (n × rating + m × platform_mean) / (n + m)

Step 2 — Combine the platforms

Adjusted ratings are averaged with weight = platform factor × ln(1 + n), so both review volume and platform reliability count.

Google 1.00  ·  TripAdvisor 0.85  ·  Yelp 0.55

Google has the deepest and most representative sample for destination attractions; Yelp is thinnest in rural Kentucky.

Step 3 — Rescale to 0–100

The combined 1–5 rating maps linearly onto 0–100 with 3.80 → 0 and 5.00 → 100, so the realistic operating band for distillery tours uses most of the scale.

The “spaghetti policy” column

A joke label over two real, separately-sourced things. Nobody publishes a spaghetti policy; what is recorded here is:

  • Can you bring your own? — whether the property has open grounds where a picnic is plausible, a full kitchen of its own, or a published rule against outside food. Only Evan Williams publishes an explicit no-outside-food-or-drink rule. Everything marked unconfirmed is genuinely unpublished, not a guess.
  • Food on site — what you can buy to eat there, from Kentucky Bourbon Trail amenity listings and distillery sites.

One hard rule, statewide: Kentucky ABC law prohibits bringing outside alcohol onto any licensed premises. No distillery can waive that. Bring the spaghetti, not the wine.

Marker shape on the chart encodes the first of these; colour still encodes stairs.

Data confidence

Flagged thin when total reviews are under 250 or fewer than two platforms carry at least 25 reviews. These plot as dashed circles.

Known limitations

  • Social media is the weakest input. There is no clean public source for post volume or sentiment, so social buzz enters only indirectly through media-coverage points and review volume. Treat the 30% as critic and press recognition first.
  • Ratings mix tour reviews with bar, restaurant and gift-shop reviews. Places with a strong on-site restaurant carry some halo they did not earn on the tour itself.
  • Drive times are Google Maps typical off-peak estimates from central Versailles, measured 11 August 2026. Add 10–20 minutes for Louisville arrivals at rush hour.
  • Accessibility is reported where a distillery or reputable source publishes it. “Unconfirmed” means not published, not inaccessible.
  • Tour prices are published ranges for standard public experiences and change frequently. Premium and private experiences can run well above the top of the range.
  • Barton 1792 is excluded: tours remain closed to the public.
